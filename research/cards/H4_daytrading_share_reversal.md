# Hypothesis card H4: reversal after extreme retail day-trading intensity

**Status: draft, pre-charter.** Not reviewed, not ranked, not committed, not tested. Predicted numbers are a-priori guesses.

*   **Claim:** Among universe stocks, those with the lowest day-trading share of volume relative to their own recent history outperform the universe over the next 10 trading days; stocks with the highest share underperform. The tradable (long-only) test is the low-share side.
*   **Mechanism:** Day trading in Taiwan is dominated by individuals, and about 97% of it in the 1990s sample (Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean). Aggregate day traders lose money net of costs, and the losses come from overconfidence and trading into momentum that has already played out. A spike in day-trading share marks attention and speculation that pushes price above fundamental value; the other side is the late, overconfident buyers. Prior caution: that evidence is old, and day-trading rules and tax were changed since.
*   **Data and `available_at`:** `TaiwanStockDayTrading` (day-trading volume), daily total volume from `prices_raw`, `universe_membership`. Published after the close, so `available_at` is the next trading day. Decision at 18:00 on the first day the 5th day of data is available, execution next open.
*   **Signal (the only free parameters, fixed here):** `dt_share = 5-day average of day-trading volume / total volume`. `z = (dt_share - own 250-day mean) / own 250-day std`. Rank `z` in the universe. Long the lowest quintile, equal weight, cap 5% per name. Rebalance every 10 trading days. The comparison group is the whole-universe equal-weight portfolio.
*   **Holding period and turnover:** 10 trading days. Attention fades quickly, so a longer hold is not the same card. Expected one-way turnover 40% to 60% per rebalance.
*   **Predicted effect and falsification:** Predicted net excess return over the equal-weight universe of 0.1% to 0.4% per month. **Falsified** on the validation window if net Sharpe after costs is below 0.5, net mean excess return over 0050 is not above zero, or the lowest quintile does not beat the highest quintile on gross 10-day return (the spread is a diagnostic of the mechanism even though only the long side trades).
*   **Prior evidence:** Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean document large and persistent day-trading losses on complete Taiwan exchange records (1995-1999 and 1992-2006 samples, from abstracts and working papers). That is evidence about trader losses, not about stock-level return predictability from a day-trading-share signal. None found for that. Not verified in this project.
*   **Dataset status:** In plan P1 (`TaiwanStockDayTrading`). Whether collected: unknown to the author of this card (data not read).
*   **Cluster:** Overlaps H5 (lottery-like, high-attention names are the same stocks) and with a short-term reversal baseline. The reviewer should check that H4 is not the same sort as "avoid recent winners".
*   **Open questions for review:** Power: with a 200-name universe and a long-only low side, the expected effect is small relative to noise. Should the low side be a quintile or an exclusion filter (drop top decile)? Is margin balance change a better retail-leverage measure, or the same idea? Does the day-trade tax cut change who day-trades?
