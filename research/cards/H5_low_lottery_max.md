# Hypothesis card H5: avoid lottery-like stocks (low MAX)

**Status: draft, pre-charter.** Not reviewed, not ranked, not committed, not tested. Predicted numbers are a-priori guesses.

*   **Claim:** Among universe stocks, those whose maximum daily return over the last 21 trading days is lowest outperform the universe over the next 20 trading days, after costs.
*   **Mechanism:** Retail investors, who trade heavily in Taiwan, overpay for stocks with lottery-like payoffs, and the daily price limit makes limit-up hits visible and attractive. Those stocks are overpriced and earn low later returns. Arbitrage is limited because shorting is constrained and costly, so the overpricing persists. The other side is retail buyers of recent big up-days. Under a long-only rule the card captures only the "do not hold the overpriced" half, which is weaker, and low MAX also loads on low volatility and large size.
*   **Data and `available_at`:** `prices_raw` and adjusted prices (daily returns), `universe_membership`. Daily prices are available after the close; decision at 18:00 on the last trading day of the month, execution next open. Returns for MAX use adjusted prices so splits and dividends do not create false big days; the limit-up count uses raw prices and `price_limits`.
*   **Signal (the only free parameters, fixed here):** `MAX = largest daily adjusted return over the last 21 trading days`. Rank in the universe. Long the lowest quintile (about 40 names), equal weight, cap 5% per name. Rebalance monthly on the last trading day.
*   **Holding period and turnover:** about 21 trading days. Expected one-way turnover 30% to 50% a month (MAX is noisy, so the sort churns).
*   **Predicted effect and falsification:** Predicted net excess return over 0050 of 0.0% to 0.4% a month, much of it possibly low-volatility beta. **Falsified** on the validation window if net Sharpe after costs is below 0.5, net mean excess return over 0050 is not above zero, or the lowest-MAX quintile does not beat the highest-MAX quintile on gross monthly return. Diagnostic, not part of the rule: regress the active return on a low-volatility sort to see how much is MAX alone.
*   **Prior evidence:** Bali, Cakici and Whitelaw (2011) document the MAX effect in the US. A Mendeley dataset linked to a Taiwan study reports that the MAX effect interacts with monthly revenue announcements and with limit-hit rates (abstract only; not read in full). Not verified in this project.
*   **Dataset status:** Only P0 (prices, price limits, universe). This is the one card that needs nothing outside the first milestone.
*   **Cluster:** Overlaps H4 and short-term reversal; overlaps low-volatility. If the reviewer finds H5 is "low volatility with a different label", it should be merged or dropped.
*   **Open questions for review:** Is the ±10% limit capping MAX, making it a coarse variable (many names tie at 10%)? Does the universe's NT$20M liquidity floor already remove the worst lottery stocks? Is the long-only version a useful test at all?
