# Hypothesis card H3: US semiconductor overnight move and the Taiwan open-to-close return

**Status: draft, pre-charter.** Not reviewed, not ranked, not committed, not tested. This card is written expecting a real chance of failing on costs; that is the point of testing it cheaply. Predicted numbers are a-priori guesses.

*   **Claim:** After a large positive (negative) US semiconductor-index session, large Taiwan electronics stocks earn a positive (negative) return from the Taiwan open to the Taiwan close the next day, beyond what the opening gap already captured, and the effect is big enough to survive costs.
*   **Mechanism:** Taiwan electronics is tied to US demand and to US index weights (TSM ADR, NVDA, AMD). The opening auction prices most of the US move. The residual is slow-moving local institutions and retail who anchor on yesterday's close and adjust through the day. The other side is those slow adjusters. Honest prior: this is the most obvious cross-market signal, so intraday arbitrage probably removes most of it, and the daily round trip is expensive.
*   **Data and `available_at`:** US prices (SOXX or SOX, TSM ADR, optionally NVDA, QQQ, VIX) and Taiwan `prices_raw` open and close. The US close on calendar day D is available before the Taiwan open on the next Taiwan trading day; align by timestamp, not by calendar date (plan section 4). Taiwan open is the entry price, close is the exit price. Limit-lock at the open: if the open is at limit-up the buy is not filled (use `price_limits`).
*   **Signal (the only free parameters, fixed here):** `us = SOXX close-to-close return on the last US session`. If `us` is at or above +1.5% go long an equal-weight basket of the 20 largest universe names tagged semiconductor or electronic components, from open to close. If at or below -1.5% hold cash (no short leg; no shorting day-trade stocks in this card). Otherwise cash.
*   **Holding period and turnover:** one trading day, open to close, on signal days only (probably 15% to 25% of days). Cost per round trip is the issue: commission both sides, sell tax (0.3%, or the reduced day-trade rate if the evaluator models it; the evaluator spec decides, not this card), and slippage at the open auction.
*   **Predicted effect and falsification:** Predicted gross open-to-close excess return on signal days of 0.1% to 0.3%, net of costs near zero. **Falsified** on the validation window if net mean return per signal day is not above zero, or net Sharpe after costs is below 0.5, or the gross open-to-close return on signal days does not exceed the gross open-to-close return on non-signal days.
*   **Prior evidence:** Customer-supplier momentum (Cohen and Frazzini, 2008) and customer-industry leading supplier-industry returns across 22 countries (Shahrur, Becker and Rosenfeld, 2010) are the closest literature, but both work at monthly or lower frequency and neither is specific to Taiwan. No intraday or open-to-close Taiwan evidence found. Not verified in this project.
*   **Dataset status:** Taiwan prices are P0. US prices are P1, source still open ("`USStockPrice` or another US source"). Whether collected: unknown to the author of this card (data not read).
*   **Cluster:** Alone. Close to a plain "gap continuation" idea; the reviewer should check whether it is just gap trading on any index.
*   **Open questions for review:** Is a daily open-to-close test a good use of a 10-backtest budget given likely cost failure? Would a lower-turnover version (use `us` to scale exposure to 0050 or to a held basket) be a different card? How does the evaluator treat the day-trade tax rate?
